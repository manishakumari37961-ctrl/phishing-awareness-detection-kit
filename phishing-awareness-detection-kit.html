Phishing Awareness & Detection Kit

Learn how to identify, avoid and report common phishing attempts.

1. What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack in which an attacker pretends to be a trusted person or organization to trick a user into revealing passwords, OTPs, bank information, or other sensitive data, or into clicking a harmful link.

Remember: A genuine organization should not ask you to share your password, OTP or recovery code through an unexpected message.

2. Common Phishing Techniques

  • Email phishing: Fake emails that imitate banks, companies or services.
  • Spear phishing: Highly targeted messages aimed at a specific person.
  • Smishing: Phishing through SMS or messaging apps.
  • Vishing: Phishing through phone calls or voice messages.
  • Social media phishing: Fake profiles, messages or support accounts.
  • QR phishing: QR codes that lead to fraudulent websites.

3. Warning Signs in Suspicious Emails

  • Urgent threats such as “your account will be closed today”.
  • Unexpected requests for passwords, OTPs, payment or personal details.
  • Unknown or mismatched sender addresses.
  • Spelling, grammar or formatting mistakes.
  • Unexpected attachments or links.
  • Offers that look too good to be true.

4. Suspicious Links and Domains

Before opening a link, check the real domain carefully. Attackers may use look-alike domains or misleading subdomains.

Example: If you expect a message from a service whose official domain is example.com, be cautious of look-alikes such as example-security.com or example.com.fake-site.test.

Tip: On a computer, hover over a link. On a phone, press and hold it to preview the destination when your app allows it. When in doubt, open the official website or app yourself instead of using the message link.

5. Fake Login Pages & Social Engineering

Fake login pages copy the appearance of real websites. Social engineering uses emotions such as fear, urgency, curiosity or trust to make people act without checking.

  • Check the website address before entering credentials.
  • Do not trust a page only because its logo and design look real.
  • Never share an OTP or authentication code with another person.
  • Pause when a message pressures you to act immediately.

6. Safe Password & Account Practices

  • Use a long, unique password for every important account.
  • Use a reputable password manager if possible.
  • Never share passwords or recovery codes.
  • Keep phone, browser and apps updated.
  • Review account login/security alerts regularly.

7. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA adds another verification step beyond the password, such as an authenticator app, security key or approved device.

Best practice: Turn on MFA for email, banking, social media and other important accounts. MFA can reduce the damage from a stolen password, although it does not make phishing impossible.

8. How to Report Suspicious Messages

  1. Do not click links, open unexpected attachments or reply.
  2. Use the email/app's Report phishing or Report spam option.
  3. For work or college accounts, inform the IT/security team.
  4. If money or sensitive information was shared, contact the relevant service/bank immediately and change affected passwords.
  5. Keep the suspicious message as evidence when safe and appropriate.

9. Realistic but Harmless Example Scenarios

Scenario A — Bank: “Your account will be blocked in 30 minutes. Verify now using this link.”
Red flags: urgency, unexpected verification, unknown link.

Scenario B — Delivery: “Your parcel could not be delivered. Pay a small fee using this link.”
Red flags: unexpected payment request and suspicious link.

Scenario C — Social media: “Your account has violated policy. Login immediately to avoid permanent suspension.”
Red flags: fear/urgency and a login link.
